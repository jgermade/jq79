## §3 - the coverage check, and it earned its keep two commits later

[`2026-08-24.clone-path-coverage-check.md`](2026-08-24.clone-path-coverage-check.md)

Every assertion in `tests/skeleton.test.ts` is of the form "the two paths
agree", and two paths agree when the second one never runs. Nothing asserted the
cloner had executed. The check that caught that in PR #12 lived in prose.

`renderFromSkeleton`'s `cloneNode(true)` is the library's only deep clone, so a
spy the test installs on the prototype answers "did the cloner run" exactly,
with nothing shipped to the browser. Each corpus entry carries `never`, `second`
or `both`, and three assertions fall out - including *plan-on-the-second-render*,
which until now had been measured once by hand in a benchmark and never
asserted.

**It caught a real regression on the first change made after it landed**:
admitting `:html` to the cloner failed the two entries that existed to prove
`:html` fell through, by name, while every DOM and order diff passed.

## §2 - MathML, and the one tag with a hyphen

[`2026-08-25.mathml.md`](2026-08-24.mathml.md) *(filed as 2026-08-24.mathml.md)*

§2 called MathML "free with the namespace change and never exercised", and said
the test would either confirm it or find the second case the `<svg>` corpus did
not cover. It found it.

`<annotation-xml>` is the only tag in either foreign namespace with a hyphen,
and a hyphen is how `mayUpgrade` recognizes a custom element. So a real MathML
element was a component-in-waiting: its `:` bindings held verbatim as parameters
and never bound, and the element itself replaced the moment anything named
`AnnotationXml` entered scope. One condition - a foreign element is never a tag
that may upgrade - and three tests that fail without it.

## §4 and §5 - one small, one that dissolved

`<template :if>` renders inert and says nothing, and a `<template :slot.x :if>`
has its directive *dropped* rather than merely ignored. Both now warn, from
`validateChains`, with the directive as the discriminant so a bare nested
`<template>` stays silent.

`:html` was parked because `:html.allowed` feeds the same effect and its warning
fires per render interpreted and would fire per definition cloned. **The
objection assumes the clone path ever sees an `:html.allowed`, and it does not**
- `plannableAttr` rejects it twice over. `:html` alone is `:text` with a
sanitizer.

## §1 - the SVG attribute table, which is not a table

[`2026-08-25.svg-attribute-names.md`](2026-08-25.svg-attribute-names.md)

§1 framed this as a product call: a table of ~55 names shipped to every page, or
the documented limitation. Both halves of the framing moved.

**There was already a spelling that worked** - `:attrs="{ viewBox: box }"`, and
it always had, because the rewrite does not reach inside an expression. The
table would have bought a spelling, not a capability.

**And there was a third option.** The HTML parser carries the table already - it
is what makes a written-out `viewBox` survive - so ask it: write the name into
markup in the element's namespace and read back what the parser called it.
Chromium 141, the build in which the IDL trick died: 58 of 62 right including
the three that killed the IDL, 0 disagreements with the document parser, 0 false
positives, 0 collisions over 58 dashed names. Verified end to end against the
built bundle - `viewBox.baseVal.width` is 40 and follows to 80, so the engine
consumes it rather than merely spelling it.

Five other routes were measured and rejected on the way, and the reasoning is
worth more than the outcome: XML parsing (every jq79 directive is a QName syntax
error), desugaring to `:attrs` at parse time, a separate renderer, dynamic
`innerHTML` (the same mechanism with the parse left in the hot path: 41× slower
and it destroys node identity), and assigning the IDL property (0 of 16 are
writable). **Vue and Svelte were read rather than remembered**: both write the
author's spelling verbatim, both have the same dead-`strokeWidth` hole, and
both can afford it because their own parser reads the template - which is
exactly Vue's documented in-DOM caveat, whose answer is "get a compiler".

## §7 - two stale files, corrected in place

`unknown-directive-warn.md`'s whole first section describes behaviour that no
longer exists. `clone-static-skeletons.md`'s note said its classifier rule "is
not safe" without saying why - it is `findComponentKey`, and a `Td` in scope
makes every `<td>` a component. Both got dated sections; neither was edited.

## §8 - a tick is not a settle, in two files

`tests/tutorial.test.ts` waited one macrotask after mounting, which is exactly
enough: measured across the tutorial, 31 of 42 mounts have content on the first
pass and 11 on the second. It waits for content now.

And the one this branch did not go looking for. A single unexplained failure
appeared once and its name was lost; thirteen plain runs could not reproduce it.
Reproducing it needed the condition - every core busy, three suites at once -
and it was **two** things: the same tick-is-not-a-settle bug in
`tests/skeleton.test.ts` (2 failures in 6 runs under load, 0 in 6 after), and a
fixed bundle path in `tests/vitePlugin.test.ts` that two concurrent vitest
processes overwrite mid-import, which is a latent hazard rather than a flake.

## Update, 2026-08-25: the branch closed at twenty commits and 909 tests

The section above was written nine commits in, at 899 tests. The branch ended at
**twenty commits, 26 files, +3,142 / -138, 909 tests**, and
[PR #13](https://github.com/jgermade/jq79/pull/13) green. What happened after
that record was written:

### A scoped review found three defects, and two of them were in the day-before fixes

[`2026-08-25.two-defects-a-review-found.md`](2026-08-25.two-defects-a-review-found.md)

`9d80bd9` and `a39f824`. Two were mine, from the previous session, and **both
are in a release** - the key-set wake in 0.6.2, 0.6.3 and 0.6.4, the debug guard
in 0.6.3 and 0.6.4:

- **`notifyReplaced` woke the `@keys` dep on the key *count*.** `{a,b}` becoming
  `{b,a}` is the same count and did not wake it. `whatChanged` already builds
  both key arrays, so `keysChanged` is a positional walk over what it had - and
  my own first fix compared union size against counts, which let the reorder
  through a second time.
- **`Component79.debug` read `key in debugFlags`**, which walks the prototype
  chain, so `debug({ toString: false })` passed the guard that existed to stop
  exactly that. `Object.prototype.hasOwnProperty.call`.
- **The `<template :slot.x :if>` warning fired in a position where the `:if` is
  honoured.** `<div><template :slot.header :if="x">` under a plain element is
  not a slot at all, so its `:if` runs. `02a6b3f` threads `parent` through
  `validateChains` and warns only when `parent?.component !== undefined`.

### The oracle's guard was wrong, and the check that would have caught it could not fail

`dc70975`. The `foreignAttrName` lookup skipped undashed names on the reasoning
that only a dashed spelling can be adjusted. `:viewbox` - lowercase, undashed -
is a real spelling an author writes, and it wrote a dead attribute while a
static `viewbox` worked. **83 undashed names measured, zero collisions**, so the
guard bought nothing and cost that.

It survived because the differential check's ground truth used
`insertAdjacentHTML`, which is the same fragment algorithm as the mechanism it
was checking. Switched to `page.setContent` - a real document parse.

### All three engines answered, and they agree name by name

`f5de857` ran for the first time on PR #13: **chromium 151, firefox 153 and
webkit 26.5, 58 of 62 names adjusted in each, and the 62 names identical across
all three**, with the mechanism reproducing each engine's own document parser
and no collision anywhere. Chromium was ten major versions past the 141 the
oracle was designed on.

Getting the cross-engine claim to be *checkable* took two more commits, and both
were the same lesson again: the matrix isolates the engines, so nothing could
compare them (`487035f`, per-engine artifacts and a `compare` job) - and that
job then failed on its first run because it imports playwright at module top
level and has no `node_modules` (`55ef2c5`, lazy import, verified from a
directory where playwright does not resolve).

### The branch's own lesson, stated once

**Four checks in this branch turned out unable to fail or unable to run**: the
vacuous differential assertion (§3 above), the fragment-parser ground truth, the
cross-engine table the matrix made unreachable, and the compare job's missing
dependency. Three of the four were written by the same hand that wrote the thing
they check.

*Write the sabotage before trusting the check.* Break it on purpose, watch it go
red, then fix it. A green check nobody has seen fail is not evidence.

### And a design conversation that produced a plan and no code

[`2026-08-25.component-tag-prefix.md`](2026-08-25.component-tag-prefix.md),
`ba1d28b` and `a5a9055`. `src/` untouched, deliberately - the plan comes first.

It started as §5's capture asymmetry and became a principle the author stated:
respect native behaviour, and never let a library component be named what the
platform names. The measurements are in the file; the ones that decided it were
that **70 of 90 ordinary one-word component names collide with a real element**,
that a warning would fire on **23 of this repo's own 28 component names**, and
that `<Tr />` is placed inside its `<tbody>` while `<Row />` is foster-parented
out - a component is positioned correctly only by accident of its name.

What is left of it is in
[`2026-08-25.open-after-pr-13.md`](2026-08-25.open-after-pr-13.md).
