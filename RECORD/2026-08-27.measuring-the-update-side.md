# The suite measures creation at 10k and updates at the noise floor

**Status:** a finding, measured before `scripts/` moved, and the change §5
proposed - which then shipped in this branch, ids and all. It starts from a
question about the published comparison page — *0.6.5 looks worse
against Vue than the previous release did* — and ends somewhere else: the
release did not regress, and the operations where jq79 beats Vue are the four
the suite measures worst.

Everything below was measured on one box (Intel Xeon @ 2.10GHz × 4, node
v22.22.2, chromium 141.0.7390.37). The published page itself could not be read
from here (network egress), so nothing in this file is a comparison against it.

---

## 1. The release did not regress

`run-ab --base v0.6.4`, 3 alternating rounds × 10 samples, after a discarded
warm-up round. `"regressions": []`.

| operation | v0.6.4 | 0.6.5 | delta | rounds agreeing |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| create1k | 30.9ms | 28.1ms | −1.4% | 3/3 |
| replace1k | 37.4ms | 33.2ms | −9.5% | 3/3 |
| selectRow | 4.3ms | 3.5ms | −16.5% | 3/3 |
| create10k | 286.2ms | 271.9ms | −9.2% | 3/3 |
| clearLarge | 111.9ms | 106.2ms | −5.7% | 2/3 |
| partialUpdate | 1.6ms | 1.6ms | +3.2% | inside the noise (±18.8%) |
| swapRows | 6.5ms | 6.7ms | +3.9% | inside the noise |
| removeRow | 5.3ms | 5.3ms | +0.9% | inside the noise |
| appendToLarge | 64.0ms | 62.3ms | −2.7% | inside the noise |

## 2. Against Vue it is a tie, and the tie is smaller than the instrument

`benchmark:comparison` twice over, same builds, same box, minutes apart. The
geometric mean the page ranks by:

| | run 1 | run 2 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| vanillajs | 1.00× | 1.00× |
| Svelte | 3.15× | 3.07× |
| **Vue** | **9.44×** | **8.79×** |
| **jq79** | **9.67×** | **9.25×** |
| Preact | 13.82× | 13.18× |
| React | 13.90× | 13.38× |

jq79/Vue: 1.024× and 1.053×. Between two runs of the *same builds* Vue's score
moved 6.9% and jq79's 4.3% — `clearLarge` alone went 111.5 → 130.4ms for jq79
with nothing changed. **The gap between the two frameworks is smaller than the
run-to-run spread of either.** The bar chart sorts by this number, so which of
the two appears above the other is a coin flip, and it is re-flipped on a
different runner at every release.

## 3. Where the suite spends its resolution

Nine operations. Three of them run on a 10,000-row table — `create10k`,
`appendToLarge`, `clearLarge` — and all three are *structural*: build a list,
grow it, tear it down. The four that touch **data** in an existing list all run
on **1,000 rows, as a single click**:

| operation | jq79 | what the clock sees |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| partialUpdate | 1.5ms | 30 timer steps; ±18.8% noise in §1 |
| selectRow | 3.0ms | 60 steps |
| removeRow | 5.7ms | mostly list teardown |
| swapRows | 6.3ms | two DOM moves |

Chromium reports `performance.now()` in 0.05ms steps. §0 of
[`2026-08-27.what-is-still-open.md`](2026-08-27.what-is-still-open.md) already
earned the rule — *an operation under ~2ms is at the instrument's resolution* —
and the official js-framework-benchmark already answers it: its update and
clear operations are repeated (`03_update10th1k_x16`, `09_clear1k_x8`). Ours
click once.

**Two consequences, and the second one is a defect.**

**(a) The score is decided by creation.** Decomposing the geometric mean into
each operation's contribution (ratio to the fastest framework in that row):

| operation | jq79 | Vue | log difference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| create10k | 6.5× | 3.2× | +0.709 |
| create1k | 6.7× | 3.7× | +0.594 |
| replace1k | 3.2× | 2.2× | +0.371 |
| clearLarge | 1.7× | 1.4× | +0.243 |
| swapRows | 63.5× | 55.0× | +0.144 |
| appendToLarge | 13.9× | 13.3× | +0.046 |
| removeRow | 37.7× | 40.7× | −0.077 |
| selectRow | 30.5× | 48.0× | −0.453 |
| **partialUpdate** | **3.0×** | **11.7×** | **−1.361** |

`partialUpdate` is jq79's largest advantage over Vue anywhere in the table —
and it is the worst-measured number of the nine.

**(b) The regression gate cannot see them.** `run-ab.mjs` sets
`GATE_MIN_MS = 5`, so an operation whose base median is under 5ms is never a
regression candidate. On this box `partialUpdate` (1.6ms) and `selectRow`
(3.5ms) are both under it. **A regression in jq79's two best operations cannot
fail a build today.**

## 4. What the update side says when it is measured above the floor

The same apps, already built, with one batch of 16 clicks timed inside the page
(the `_x16` shape). Medians of 5 fresh page loads, ms:

| operation | jq79 | vanillajs | Svelte | Vue | React | Preact |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| update every 10th, 1k rows ×16 | **16.6** | 1.9 | 8.3 | 49.5 | 63.3 | 67.7 |
| update every 10th, **10k rows** ×16 | **104.2** | 25.5 | 62.7 | 567.5 | 586.1 | 894.3 |
| select a row, 1k rows ×16 | **27.9** | 0.5 | 6.0 | 42.9 | 54.6 | 66.8 |
| select a row, 10k rows ×16 | **319.7** | 0.4 | 40.3 | 418.5 | 630.4 | 774.3 |
| swap rows, 1k ×16 | 51.2 | 0.4 | 6.8 | 53.4 | 111.1 | 65.1 |

jq79 against Vue: **0.18×** on the 10k update, 0.34× on the 1k update, 0.65×
and 0.76× on select, 0.96× on swap. It beats Vue, React and Preact on every one
of them, and on the 10k update it is within **1.7×** of Svelte — against 3.5×
on `create1k` and 4.9× on `create10k`.

That is the shape of the library stated plainly: **an interpreter pays on the
way in and collects on every write afterwards.** The suite currently measures
the paying at 10k and the collecting at 1k.

## 5. Proposed

Not "add operations jq79 wins". The 10k update is the cell the suite is missing
by its own symmetry — it builds at 10k, appends at 10k and clears at 10k, and
then updates at 1k — and the repetition is the fix the instrument's resolution
demands whoever it flatters.

1. **New ids for the repeated shapes**, retiring the single-click ones:
   `update1k_x16`, `select1k_x16`, `swap1k_x16`.
2. **Add `update10k_x16`**, symmetric with `create10k` / `clearLarge`.
3. Leave `removeRow` alone. It is the one that cannot repeat without rebuilding
   the table between clicks, which would put the rebuild inside the
   measurement - and at 5.3ms it already clears `GATE_MIN_MS`.
4. Leave `create1k`, `replace1k`, `create10k`, `appendToLarge`, `clearLarge`
   untouched, ids and meaning alike.

A batch is only worth what its clicks actually did, so each one now asserts it
after the clock stops: 16 update marks on row 1, one `tr.danger` after the
select walk, the row count after the rest. A batch clicking detached nodes does
no work and reports a very good time for it; that is the failure the assertions
are there to catch.

**New ids, not new meanings under the old ids — and the first reason given here
for that was wrong.** It said the *regression* workflow compares head against
the last released tag by operation id, so a `partialUpdate` that kept its id and
became a batch of 16 would read as a 16× regression on the next PR, and a
retired id would leave the new operation without a base for one release.

Neither happens. `run-ab.mjs` takes only `dist/` from the base ref — `buildInto`
runs `tsup` in the base worktree and copies the built library, nothing else. The
benchmark app and `lib/benchmark-ops.mjs` both come from the head checkout, so
**both builds are measured live, with the same operation set.** A new id has a
base from the first run, and a redefined one would have been measured as 16
clicks on both sides. The gate covers `update1k_x16` and the rest immediately.

The reason that survives is the stored data. Every result file, every page built
from one, and a good half of the numbers quoted in this directory are keyed by
operation id — eighteen notes in this directory name `partialUpdate`, most of
them beside a number. An id that quietly starts measuring something 16 times
larger makes numbers from either side of the change look comparable when they
are not. A retired id makes that impossible to get wrong.

**What it costs.** The per-release numbers stored for the retired ids stop
continuing, and the page's geometric mean changes composition, so scores before
and after the change are not comparable to each other. Both are one-time, and
both are already true every time the comparison runs on a different runner.

**What it does not do.** It does not touch `src/`. It does not close the
creation gap, which §3 of
[`2026-08-27.what-is-still-open.md`](2026-08-27.what-is-still-open.md) says is
the price of the design. And it does not make jq79 beat Vue on the overall
score — on these numbers it would, but that is an outcome of the change, not
its argument.

## 6. What the suite says after the change

Regenerated on this box with the ten operations above (medians, ms):

| operation | jq79 | vanillajs | Svelte | Vue | React | Preact |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| create1k | 28.7 | 4.9 | 9.3 | 17.1 | 18.6 | 22.2 |
| replace1k | 32.5 | 10.0 | 14.2 | 21.7 | 24.8 | 31.9 |
| update1k_x16 | 11.4 | 2.0 | 8.0 | 50.0 | 57.5 | 72.2 |
| select1k_x16 | 27.5 | 0.3 | 6.5 | 40.7 | 54.8 | 69.5 |
| swap1k_x16 | 51.7 | 0.3 | 7.0 | 45.0 | 119.5 | 66.5 |
| removeRow | 5.7 | 0.2 | 1.1 | 5.9 | 10.7 | 8.0 |
| create10k | 266.9 | 42.5 | 57.6 | 144.3 | 253.6 | 161.4 |
| update10k_x16 | 107.3 | 22.9 | 62.0 | 482.4 | 676.3 | 847.4 |
| appendToLarge | 61.5 | 4.2 | 10.8 | 56.8 | 71.5 | 110.6 |
| clearLarge | 109.0 | 63.4 | 73.2 | 86.3 | 112.3 | 93.3 |
| **geometric mean** | **11.34×** | 1.00× | 3.56× | 13.07× | 18.77× | 18.84× |

jq79 now sits ahead of Vue on the page's score, 11.34× against 13.07×. **That is
the outcome of the change, not its argument** - §5 said so before the numbers
existed, and it is worth repeating next to them. What actually moved is that the
suite stopped reporting jq79's best operations at 30 timer steps, and started
measuring an update at the scale it already measured every other 10,000-row
operation. Vue's own numbers did not get worse; they got measured.

The scale of the score changed for everyone (every framework's ratio is against
vanillajs, whose select and swap are 0.3ms), so **11.34× is not comparable to
the 9.67× the nine-operation suite reported.** Scores from before this change
and after it are different units.

`removeRow` came out at 5.0-5.7ms on this box, which is the wrong side of
comfortable against `GATE_MIN_MS = 5`. If it drifts under, it leaves the gate's
reach and has to be repeated too - rebuilding the table between clicks, outside
the clock.

## 7. The gate path, exercised on the new suite

`run-ab --base v0.6.4`, 2 rounds × 10 samples — the same path the *regression*
workflow takes. It confirms §5's correction from the other end: v0.6.4's `dist`
was measured under `update1k_x16` and `select1k_x16`, ids that did not exist
when it was tagged, because the operations come from the head checkout.

| operation | v0.6.4 | 0.6.5 | delta | noise | verdict |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| create1k | 34.4ms | 26.9ms | −21.8% | ±10.2% | faster, every round |
| replace1k | 40.0ms | 32.9ms | −17.7% | ±8.8% | faster, every round |
| update1k_x16 | 12.6ms | 11.3ms | −9.9% | ±15.5% | under the noise |
| **select1k_x16** | **45.4ms** | **27.1ms** | **−40.1%** | **±5.5%** | faster, every round |
| swap1k_x16 | 57.3ms | 55.9ms | −2.3% | ±9.2% | inside the noise |
| removeRow | 5.4ms | 5.1ms | −6.0% | ±5.6% | faster, every round |
| create10k | 282.3ms | 255.4ms | −9.5% | ±5.4% | faster, every round |
| update10k_x16 | 124.9ms | 116.5ms | −6.5% | ±14.8% | inside the noise |
| appendToLarge | 64.7ms | 64.3ms | −0.7% | ±11.4% | inside the noise |
| clearLarge | 118.0ms | 113.3ms | −3.9% | ±6.4% | under the noise |

**The repetition bought exactly what it was for.** The same comparison on the
single-click suite (§1) put `selectRow` at −16.5% with ±12.9% of noise beside
it. As a batch of 16 it is **−40.1% against ±5.5%** — a delta seven times its
own noise, where before it was barely over one. Nothing about the two builds
changed between those two runs; only the instrument did. That is what
`scopedNames` was worth on a select all along, and the old measurement could not
say it.

The two `×16` update operations still carry ±15% noise, which is the batch
running long enough to catch the runner's own weather. More rounds is the only
answer to that, and `--only update10k_x16` is how to afford them.
