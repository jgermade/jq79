# `:html` in the cloner, and the warning that was the whole objection

**Status:** proposal, then implementation. It is §5 of
[`2026-08-24.open-after-pr-12.md`](2026-08-24.open-after-pr-12.md), which
records `:html` as *"parked with a reason rather than forgotten"* and asks for
its own decision.

The reason does not survive contact with `plannableAttr`.

---

## What the objection was

[`2026-08-24.more-holes-in-the-cloner.md`](2026-08-24.more-holes-in-the-cloner.md)
admitted five directives to the skeleton and left `:html` out:

> `:html.allowed` is a second attribute feeding the same effect, and the warning
> renderNode emits for an `:html.allowed` with no `:html` fires once per
> *render* there and would fire once per *definition* here. That is a real
> difference in observable behaviour for a directive whose whole job is
> sanitizing untrusted input.

Correct, and it assumes the clone path would have to see an `:html.allowed` at
all. It never does.

## Why the objection dissolves

`plannableAttr` rejects `:html.allowed` twice over - it is in `CONTROL_ATTRS`
and it has a dot in it, and either clause is enough. Admitting `:html` alone
does not change that. So:

| written | plan | who warns |
|---|---|---|
| `<p :html="v">` | cloned | nobody: there is no `:html.allowed` to warn about |
| `<p :html="v" :html.allowed="policy">` | **unplannable**, interpreted | unchanged |
| `<p :html.allowed="policy">` | **unplannable**, interpreted | renderNode, once per render, unchanged |

The warning is emitted from `renderNode`, and every element that can possibly
emit it is still rendered by `renderNode`. There is no second place for it to
fire from, and therefore no decision to make about how often.

What is left is the ordinary question the other five answered: does the clone
path produce the same DOM. `:html` is `:text` with a sanitizer - it replaces the
element's content, the children the source wrote inside it are built by neither
path, and the sanitizing happens inside the effect, per instance, on both. The
op is `textContent`'s twin.

## The change

Five edits, each the sibling of one `:text` already has:

- `PLANNABLE_CONTROL_ATTRS` gains `":html"`.
- `plannableNode` and `countElements` treat an `:html` element as a leaf, as
  they do a `:text` one - what the source wrote inside it is markup nobody
  renders.
- `buildSkeleton` pushes `{ kind: "html", path, expr }` where it pushes
  `textContent`, in the same else-if order `renderNode` uses (`:text` wins when
  both are written).
- `renderFromSkeleton` grows the branch, which is `sanitizeHTML` around what
  `textContent` does.

The cost of leaving `:html.allowed` off the list is that an element carrying one
keeps its whole subtree interpreted. That is slower and correct, and it is the
rule this area already runs on.

## What was rejected

- **Admitting `:html.allowed` too**, with the warning moved into the op-application
  loop so it fires per instance. It would work - the ops run per instance, which
  is per render - and it buys the clone path an element that pairs a sanitizer
  policy with content. Not taken: it puts a diagnostic about a security-relevant
  misuse in two places, to speed up the rarer of the two spellings, with no
  measurement asking for it. The version above needs no such argument.
- **A plan-time warning.** The thing the parked note was right to refuse.

## What it measures

Nothing, and deliberately: the benchmark row has no `:html` in it, so
`benchmark:ab` cannot say anything about this. What it buys is coverage - a
subtree containing an `:html` goes from wholly interpreted to planned - and the
differential test is where that is visible.

## What it moves in the corpus

`tests/skeleton.test.ts` carried `:html` in `SHAPE_CORPUS` as a trip wire, and
`:text and :html` in `CORPUS` labelled `never`. Both were true because `:html`
made its subtree unplannable, and both stop being true here. They move rather
than being deleted - the same relocation `<svg>` made when it stopped falling
through - and a **new** trip wire takes the old one's place: `:html.allowed`,
which must go on making a subtree unplannable, and which is now the only half of
the pair that does.

The coverage assertions added in
[`2026-08-24.clone-path-coverage-check.md`](2026-08-24.clone-path-coverage-check.md)
are what force this: with the corpus untouched, admitting `:html` fails two
entries by name - "this entry is a trip wire and it just became plannable" -
rather than passing quietly with two fewer entries reaching the cloner. That is
the first time that check has been the thing that noticed, and it noticed on the
first change made after it landed.
